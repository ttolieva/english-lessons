I Used to Be
a City Girl

Lifestyle · 5 min read

For seven years, Sofia lived on the ninth floor of a in a of four million people. Her street was and pavement cafés, and the traffic was from seven in the morning until late at night. “I used to fall asleep to the of buses,” she laughs. “I didn't even notice it. I was used to it.”

Then, two years ago, she started working remotely and made a decision her friends called crazy: she rented a tiny cottage on the edge of a , forty minutes from the nearest town. The cottage is surrounded by , and a stream through the trees at the end of the garden. “The first night was terrible,” Sofia admits. “It was so quiet that I couldn't sleep. I kept waiting for a car to go past.”

Getting used to country life took time. She had to get used to driving everywhere, to shops that close at six, and to the dark — real dark, with no street lights. “In the city I used to order food at midnight. Here, if you forget to buy bread, you don't eat bread,” she says. But slowly, the rhythm of the countryside changed her. She stopped checking her phone every five minutes. She started going for long walks and noticing things: the smell of the forest after the rain, the light at sunset — and the deer.

Because there are deer. Almost every morning, a family of fallow deer comes out of the forest to the edge of the garden. “The first time I a , I tried to , but it me and disappeared,” Sofia smiles. “Now they're used to me. I'm used to them too — but I still stop whatever I'm doing to watch them.”

Does she miss the city? “Sometimes I miss the , my friends, the feeling that something is always happening,” she says. “And no, I'll never get used to spiders in the bathroom. But when I go back to the city now, I can't stand the and the noise. I used to love that energy. Now I need more than I need a pizza at 2 a.m.”

Check the meanings
  1. high-rise block — a tall building with many floors
  2. sprawling city — spread over a very large area (slightly negative)
  3. lined with shops — with shops all along both sides
  4. bumper-to-bumper — so many cars that they almost touch each other
  5. incessant roar — a very loud noise that never stops
  6. dense forest — a thick forest — the trees grow very close together
  7. open fields — fields with no woods or houses in them
  8. winds — makes lots of bends, doesn't go in a straight line
  9. tranquil — calm and peaceful
  10. caught a glimpse of — saw something for a very short moment
  11. fawn — a baby deer
  12. take a picture — to take a photo
  13. caught sight of — suddenly saw (me)
  14. lively bars — full of people, music and energy
  15. exhaust fumes — the gases that come out of cars and buses
  16. breathtaking scenery — nature so beautiful that it takes your breath away

Grammar≈ 15 min

used to · be used to · get used to. Three phrases that look the same and mean different things. Work it out yourself first, then open the rule.

Sofia's words
  1. I used to fall asleep to the roar of buses.
  2. I didn't notice it. I was used to it.
  3. She had to get used to driving everywhere.
  4. Now the deer are used to me.
Which sentence is about a past habit that isn't true now?
Check
Sentence 1 — used to + infinitive (used to fall).
Which ones mean “it's normal for me, not strange”?
Check
Sentences 2 and 4 — be used to + noun/pronoun.
Which one describes a process — something slowly becoming normal?
Check
Sentence 3 — get used to.
What comes after be / get used to: drive or driving?
Check
driving (or a noun). Here to is a preposition, so the verb takes -ing.
